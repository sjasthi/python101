1. Math comparisons

A comparison asks how two values relate. Its answer is either true (yes) or false (no). Compare points, ages, temperatures, or lengths using the same units.

SymbolMeaningExampleResult
=equal to8 = 8True (yes)
≠not equal to8 ≠ 3True (yes)
>greater than8 > 3True (yes)
<less than3 < 8True (yes)
≥greater than or equal to8 ≥ 8True (yes)
≤less than or equal to3 ≤ 8True (yes)
Read from left to right. 3 < 8 says “3 is less than 8.” The open side faces the larger number; the pointed end faces the smaller number.

Does equality count?

“At least 10” includes 10: use ≥. “At most 10” also includes 10: use ≤. “More than 10” uses > and excludes 10.

10 ≥ 10 is true
10 > 10 is false
−2 > −5 is true

Think before you peek

Are these true or false? 6 < 4 • 5 ≤ 5 • 9 ≠ 9 • 2.5 > 2

Reveal the reasoning

False: 6 is larger than 4. True: equality is allowed. False: both values are 9. True: 2.5 is larger than 2.

Conditions choose a path
A condition is an expression that can be checked as true or false. When the condition is True, Python runs the indented if block. Otherwise, it runs the else block. Exactly one of these two blocks runs.

4. Indentation: spaces tell the story
In Python, indentation is part of the meaning of your program. It tells Python which lines belong to a condition. Move a line in or out of a block and you can change what happens!
Four helpful rules
  • Use four spaces for each level of indentation as standard practice.
  • Lines in the same block line up. Be consistent; avoid mixing tabs and spaces.
  • Align else and elif with their matching if.
  • A colon starts a block. Its body must be indented. Moving back to the left ends that block.
Same words, different behavior

Explain the difference
With 5 points, A prints nothing: both messages belong to the False condition. B prints “Thanks for playing” because the last line is outside the block. With 10 points, both programs print both messages. Valid indentation can change behavior even when there is no error.
